# Final Investor Demo Polish

## Final demo path

1. `/` — Position Cyber Sentinels as operational trust infrastructure.
2. `/trust-center` — Show how workflow trust, evidence and governance stay connected. This surface requires an authenticated account.
3. `/enterprise/hiring-security` — Introduce the hiring-security use case and the operational problem.
4. `/demo/hiring-attack` — Run the controlled synthetic-applicant scenario.
5. `/verify/session` — Explain session evidence and why a signal is not a verdict.
6. `/replay/demo` — Reconstruct what entered, what evidence existed, how trust changed, where governance intervened and the final outcome.
7. `/verification/receipt/demo` — Show the portable record of evidence, review and outcome.
8. `/status/verification` — Establish provider and validation boundaries.
9. `/enterprise-access` — Close with one reviewable design-partner workflow.

## What to say

- “Cyber Sentinels makes trust changes explainable across an operational workflow.”
- “The system connects identity and session evidence with human governance, replay and a final receipt.”
- “This scenario is simulated so the evidence boundary is explicit.”
- “Replay shows what changed, why the workflow was restricted and who owned the intervention.”
- “Provider signals inform the decision; they do not become the decision.”
- “A design partnership starts with one exposed workflow and one accountable review owner.”

## What not to claim

- Do not claim perfect real/fake, deepfake, biometric or fraud detection.
- Do not describe simulated evidence as a live provider result.
- Do not claim a provider is integrated or available when its state is Awaiting Credentials or Disabled.
- Do not call a receipt legal proof or a definitive identity verdict.
- Do not imply that automated scoring replaces human governance.
- Do not promise benchmark performance that has not been independently measured.

## Provider status language

- **Live** — Configured and available for its stated workflow purpose.
- **Simulated** — Controlled demonstration data only; no live provider result is claimed.
- **Awaiting Credentials** — Integration support exists, but required credentials are not configured.
- **Disabled** — Not active in the current environment or workflow.

Use the exact state shown on `/status/verification`. Describe a provider’s purpose separately from its availability.

## Known limitations

- The investor scenario uses controlled, simulated evidence.
- Provider availability depends on the deployed environment and approved credentials.
- Provider evidence is normalized into the workflow; it does not independently establish identity or authenticity.
- Detection and scoring outputs require representative validation before accuracy claims can be made.
- Protected trust-center and operational records require authentication.
- Replay and receipts explain retained workflow evidence; they cannot reconstruct evidence that was never captured.

## Investor-safe answers

**Is this a deepfake detector?**  
No. Detection may be one input. Cyber Sentinels coordinates evidence, workflow trust, governance and replay so a sensitive decision remains explainable.

**What is proprietary?**  
The product value is the operational trust layer: how evidence, state changes, authorization, review ownership, replay and receipts remain connected across a workflow. Do not imply ownership of third-party provider models.

**What works today?**  
The walkthrough demonstrates the working trust workflow, replay, receipt, governance language and fail-closed provider states. The status page identifies which external sources are Live, Simulated, Awaiting Credentials or Disabled in the current environment.

**How accurate is it?**  
No universal accuracy claim is made. Accuracy belongs to a defined provider, dataset, threshold and test condition. Cyber Sentinels preserves those inputs and their limitations for accountable review.

**Why does governance matter?**  
Because sensitive outcomes need a named authority, a recorded intervention and evidence that can be reviewed after the decision.

**What would a design partner test?**  
One exposed workflow, its evidence boundary, failure states, review ownership, replay usefulness and the final receipt with real stakeholders under an agreed protocol.
