# No-blueprint public content policy

## Purpose

Public content may explain the enterprise problem, the Operational Trust Intelligence™ category and the intended outcome. It must never give a reader enough implementation detail to reconstruct the product.

Public claims must also align with `docs/CYBER_SENTINELS_CAPABILITY_TRUTH_MATRIX.md`. When evidence is incomplete, use restrained wording such as “building,” “developing,” “designed to,” “design-partner stage,” or “pilot stage” where proven.

## Content classes

### PUBLIC

May include:

- the category and enterprise problem;
- high-level enterprise outcomes;
- approved capability names used only as capability names;
- approved positioning;
- the value of customer-controlled evidence, accountability and historical proof;
- design-partner, enterprise-pilot and early-investor invitations.

### CONTROLLED

May be shared only under NDA or controlled investor access:

- limited product demonstrations;
- selected screenshots cleared for the audience;
- implementation-backed capability evidence;
- high-level platform relationships;
- roadmap themes without confidential sequencing.

### CONFIDENTIAL

Must not be publicly exposed:

- source code or prompts;
- schemas, algorithms or migrations;
- internal platform or security design;
- provider contracts or credentials;
- exact decision, ranking, policy or reason-code logic;
- internal roadmap or implementation sequence;
- unreleased integration details;
- vulnerability information.

## Permitted public questions

- What changed?
- Why did trust change?
- Who authorized the action?
- Which evidence supported the decision?
- Who was accountable?
- What happened next?
- Can the organization prove it?

Public content may ask these questions but must not explain the internal mechanism that answers them.

## Publication review

Before approving any public content, answer every question below:

- Does it expose implementation?
- Does it reveal the internal workflow?
- Does it reveal a data model?
- Does it explain an algorithm?
- Does it reveal exact policy logic?
- Does it expose internal screenshots?
- Does it disclose unreleased providers?
- Does it reveal security controls?
- Does it disclose roadmap sequencing?
- Does it contain unsupported claims?
- Could a competitor reconstruct the product from this?
- Does it claim more than the code currently proves?

If any answer creates material risk, block publication.

## Public copy guardrails

Do not publish architecture diagrams, system flows, internal object or service names, database terminology, event taxonomies, trust-state transitions, reason-code lists, implementation prompts, internal work-item names, provider-normalization details, security enforcement logic, unreleased screenshots or technical competitive detail.

Do not use calls to action that invite readers to see the mechanism, architecture, algorithm, blueprint or full system demonstration.

Do not claim production deployment, enterprise-scale proof, continuous live monitoring, legal compliance, regulatory approval, autonomous decision-making, guaranteed detection, perfect attribution or predictive certainty unless the truth matrix is updated with independently reviewable evidence and the claim passes legal review.

Use ™ only as a claimed brand designation. Never use a registered trademark symbol or say a mark is registered unless documentary legal evidence exists.
