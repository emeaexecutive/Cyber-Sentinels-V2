# Cyber Sentinels Trust Primitives

Cyber Sentinels is built around eight permanent trust primitives. Together they define how Operational Trust remains explainable across humans, AI agents and enterprise workflows.

Each primitive is part of one continuity model. None should be presented as an isolated product or disconnected trust brand.

## 1. Trust Posture

### What it means

Trust Posture is the current, explainable operational state of a human, AI agent or workflow. It is derived from identity, evidence, authorization, Session Integrity and Governance Review within a defined context.

### Why enterprises need it

Enterprises need to know whether a subject or workflow remains current, requires reverification, carries elevated indicators or is waiting for accountable review. A one-time verification cannot answer that after context changes.

### How it evolves

Trust Posture can strengthen, degrade, become stale or enter Governance Review as new evidence, authorization changes, session events and reviewer decisions accumulate.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay Timeline reconstructs every retained transition behind the current posture. Governance Review records the human intervention that approved, restricted or escalated the workflow.

## 2. Replay Timeline

### What it means

Replay Timeline is the canonical, read-only chronology of Operational Trust. It connects evidence, posture transitions, authorization changes, governance actions and final outcomes.

### Why enterprises need it

Current status is not enough for high-consequence work. Enterprises need to reconstruct what happened, which evidence was available, who or what acted, who intervened and why the outcome followed.

### How it evolves

Replay grows as operational records are retained. New events extend the chronology without rewriting earlier evidence or governance history.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay is the operational memory layer. Governance Review appears inside the chronology with reviewer ownership, rationale and resolution rather than in a disconnected approval history.

## 3. Evidence Chain

### What it means

Evidence Chain is the retained set of source records, provider signals, workflow references, timestamps and review context available to support an operational decision.

### Why enterprises need it

Decisions must remain attributable to the evidence available at the time. Evidence Chain prevents summaries or scores from becoming detached from their sources and limitations.

### How it evolves

Evidence can be added, refreshed, superseded or become stale. Its provenance and time boundary remain visible.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay Timeline orders evidence beside the transition it informed. Governance Review records how a reviewer used—or declined to rely on—that evidence.

## 4. Governance Review

### What it means

Governance Review is named human authority over sensitive trust transitions. It preserves escalation reason, accountable owner, intervention, rationale and resolution.

### Why enterprises need it

High-impact workflows cannot rely on opaque automation. Enterprises need clear ownership when evidence conflicts, authorization changes or posture degrades.

### How it evolves

A review can be opened, assigned, escalated, resolved or reopened as new evidence changes the operational context.

### Replay and governance connection

Every Governance Review action becomes part of Replay Timeline. The final outcome stays connected to who intervened, under which authority and with what evidence.

## 5. Authorization Lineage

### What it means

Authorization Lineage is the history of grants, delegated authority, permission changes, policy boundaries and revocations that explain what an actor was allowed to do.

### Why enterprises need it

Identity alone does not establish authority. Enterprises need to understand whether an action occurred within current permission scope and who was accountable for that scope.

### How it evolves

Authorization can be granted, narrowed, expanded, delegated, suspended or revoked as role, policy and Trust Posture change.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay Timeline places authorization changes beside actual workflow execution. Governance Review records the authority behind sensitive exceptions or restrictions.

## 6. Session Integrity

### What it means

Session Integrity is the evidence-backed condition of the active channel, device and interaction context. It remains distinct from identity verification.

### Why enterprises need it

A verified identity does not guarantee that a later session or channel remains trustworthy. Enterprises need observable session changes as review context without turning them into automatic identity verdicts.

### How it evolves

Session Integrity changes as device, channel, interruption, injection-risk or workflow context changes. Indicators can return to normal, require step-up verification or trigger Governance Review.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay Timeline shows when Session Integrity changed and which evidence existed. Governance Review determines whether the workflow can continue, must be restricted or requires stronger evidence.

## 7. Continuous Verification

### What it means

Continuous Verification is the use of meaningful, consent-based checkpoints after initial access. It keeps identity, ownership, evidence and operating context current.

### Why enterprises need it

Trust can expire while access persists. Enterprises need reverification when material context changes, not hidden continuous surveillance.

### How it evolves

Verification freshness moves from current to checkpoint, reverification due or Governance Review based on retained evidence and policy.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay Timeline explains why a checkpoint occurred and what changed afterward. Governance Review remains authoritative where verification is incomplete or conflicting.

## 8. Operational Memory

### What it means

Operational Memory is the persistent relationship between Trust Posture, Evidence Chain, Authorization Lineage, Governance Review, Session Integrity and final outcome.

### Why enterprises need it

Enterprise workflows cross people, agents, systems and time. Without operational memory, trust context fragments and decisions become difficult to explain or audit.

### How it evolves

Operational Memory accumulates as linked workflow events are retained. It preserves historical state without treating old evidence as automatically current.

### Replay and governance connection

Replay Timeline is the interaction model for Operational Memory. Governance Review gives that memory accountable human meaning and authority.

## The permanent continuity model

The primitives work in one sequence:

1. Continuous Verification establishes current identity and context.
2. Evidence Chain records what is available.
3. Authorization Lineage defines the operating boundary.
4. Session Integrity tracks active workflow context.
5. Trust Posture expresses the current state.
6. Governance Review owns sensitive transitions.
7. Replay Timeline reconstructs the chronology.
8. Operational Memory preserves continuity across the full workflow.

## Language standard

Use these names consistently:

- Operational Trust
- Persistent Trust Posture
- Replay Timeline
- Evidence Chain
- Governance Review
- Authorization Lineage
- Session Integrity
- Continuous Verification
- Operational Memory

Do not create additional branded trust primitives when one of these terms already describes the concept.
