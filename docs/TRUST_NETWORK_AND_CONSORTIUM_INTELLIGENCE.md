# Trust Network and Consortium Intelligence

## Purpose

Cyber Sentinels helps organizations understand operational trust continuity and
emerging workflow risk patterns across intelligent systems. Consortium
intelligence is designed as a privacy-preserving coordination layer for
operational patterns, not a shared identity database.

The initial foundation aggregates authorized workflow observations already used
for validation and benchmarking. It does not create a new data collection
system, expose cross-organization identities or autonomously change an
enterprise workflow.

## Privacy philosophy

Operational coordination must disclose less data than the source workflow.
Network outputs therefore:

- contain category-level counts and trends;
- suppress cohorts smaller than the configured minimum;
- exclude workflow and evidence identifiers;
- exclude names, contact details and identity attributes;
- exclude raw biometric and behavioral data;
- separate controlled simulations from authorized operational records; and
- remain subject to the querying user's existing authentication and row-level
  access.

Absence of a visible count can mean insufficient evidence or privacy
suppression. It must not be interpreted as proof that a pattern did or did not
occur.

## Consortium intelligence boundaries

The consortium foundation supports:

- repeated workflow anomaly categories;
- provider degradation observations;
- workflow interruption trends;
- session-integrity instability;
- replay divergence frequency; and
- governance escalation density.

It explicitly does not support:

- public identity scoring;
- identity sharing between organizations;
- raw biometric sharing;
- personal or behavioral profiling;
- organization league tables;
- automatic accusations; or
- automatic punitive action.

Consortium participation, tenancy isolation, contractual controls and any
future privacy technology require separate design and validation before
cross-organization operation.

## Operational signal sharing

Each aggregated signal explains:

- what changed;
- why the category matters;
- which evidence category contributed;
- whether the source is provider-backed, governance-derived, replay-derived,
  simulated or aggregated; and
- the operational advisory an accountable reviewer can consider.

Signals are advisories. They do not establish authenticity, fraud, intent or
identity certainty.

## Provider reliability awareness

Provider summaries use normalized workflow outcomes and only appear when the
provider cohort meets the aggregation threshold. They describe operational
history, not provider accuracy, quality rankings or contractual availability.

Provider-backed evidence remains one input to governance. A provider failure
may indicate configuration, availability, unsupported evidence or workflow
conditions; it is not proof about a person.

## Replay anomaly awareness

Replay remains canonical operational evidence. Aggregated context can later
help an authorized reviewer understand whether a replay divergence or workflow
interruption belongs to a broader operational category.

Network context must not:

- insert external identities into a replay;
- rewrite the retained chronology;
- obscure the source evidence;
- replace reviewer reasoning; or
- turn a pattern into an accusation.

Replay should retain an advisory reference and category when appropriate, while
the underlying decision remains based on authorized workflow evidence.

## Governance continuity

Governance escalation density measures use of human oversight, not wrongdoing.
Consortium advisories should help enterprises assess reviewer capacity, policy
clarity and recurring control friction.

Every governance action remains attributable inside the originating
organization. Aggregated network outputs must not expose reviewer identities or
workflow ownership across organizations.

## Simulated indicators

Controlled fixtures test aggregation, cohort suppression, explanation and UI
behavior. Simulated synthetic candidate, provider instability, replay
divergence, governance-chain and session-integrity observations are always
displayed separately from authorized operational evidence.

Simulation counts are not detection benchmarks or production threat
prevalence.

## Ethical limitations

Trust network intelligence must remain proportionate, explainable and
governance-safe. Cyber Sentinels does not use this layer for social ranking,
predictive policing, workforce surveillance or automated accusations.

Future consortium capabilities require:

1. explicit organizational participation and purpose limitation;
2. tenant-isolation and privacy review;
3. documented aggregation and suppression rules;
4. retention and deletion controls;
5. adversarial and re-identification testing;
6. accountable governance ownership; and
7. independent legal, security and ethical review.

Until those controls are validated, the product should present consortium
intelligence as an internal, privacy-preserving operational foundation.
