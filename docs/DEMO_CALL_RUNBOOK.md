# Demo Call Runbook

## 15-minute demo structure

### 0:00-2:00 - Set the context

Open with the problem: AI has made it easier for fake applicants, proxy interviewers, and injected sessions to look legitimate. The buyer is not only trying to detect media manipulation. They need a defensible way to review what happened inside a sensitive workflow.

Use the line: "Cyber Sentinels helps protect enterprise workflows against synthetic identity attacks by turning verification, session integrity, governance review, replay, and receipts into one operational trust record."

### 2:00-4:00 - Show the workflow first

Start with `/demo`, then move into `/demo/hiring-attack`. Do not begin in an admin dashboard. The first impression should be the buyer's operational story:

- A fake candidate enters the workflow.
- Verification begins.
- Session Integrity checks start.
- Risk appears during the session.
- Governance Review opens.

### 4:00-8:00 - Walk the hiring attack

Narrate the journey in plain language:

- Identity is submitted.
- Human presence is checked.
- Session integrity is checked.
- Injection risk events appear.
- The case escalates to Governance Review.
- A reviewer action is recorded.
- A receipt is issued.

Keep the language evidence-first. Avoid claims that the system guarantees trust or replaces reviewer judgment.

### 8:00-11:00 - Explain replay and receipts

Move from the demo flow into Replay Evidence and Verification Receipts.

Replay should be framed as a chronology of evidence and decisions, not a surveillance feed. It helps the enterprise answer: what happened, when did risk change, who reviewed it, and what decision was made?

Receipts should be framed as printable, audit-ready summaries. They show trust state, reviewer action, chronology, replay references, and verification evidence without exposing unnecessary internal data.

### 11:00-13:00 - Connect to pilot value

Explain how a design partner pilot would validate the workflow with the buyer's real risk model:

- What counts as elevated risk?
- Which events should trigger Governance Review?
- What evidence should appear in a receipt?
- Who needs to approve the final outcome?

### 13:00-15:00 - Close and next step

Ask whether the workflow maps to their current concern. If yes, propose a focused design partner session with talent, security, and compliance stakeholders.

Recommended next step: "Let's schedule a 45-minute workflow review with the people who handle suspicious candidates today. We can map your current process to the trust journey and decide whether a pilot is useful."

## What to show first

Show the public demo first, not protected operational pages.

Recommended order:

1. `/demo`
2. `/demo/hiring-attack`
3. `/demo/session-integrity`
4. Replay Evidence or receipt view, using demo-safe examples
5. `/enterprise-access` or `/design-partner` as the next step

## How to explain replay

Replay is the operational chronology. It is not a promise that every attack is detected, and it is not surveillance positioning.

Use this explanation:

"Replay shows the evidence sequence behind the decision. It lets a reviewer see when verification started, when session integrity changed, what flags appeared, when Governance Review opened, what action was taken, and how the final outcome was issued."

## How to explain verification receipts

Verification Receipts are audit-ready summaries of a trust decision.

Use this explanation:

"A receipt gives the enterprise a printable record of the final trust state. It includes the relevant evidence, reviewer action, chronology, replay reference, and outcome. The point is to make the decision explainable later without exposing more data than necessary."

## Handling: "Is this just deepfake detection?"

Answer:

"Deepfake detection can be one signal, but Cyber Sentinels is broader. The product connects identity verification, human presence, Session Integrity, injection risk, Governance Review, replay, and receipts. The value is not only detecting suspicious media. It is helping the enterprise review the workflow and record an accountable decision."

## Handling: "How is this different from Mimecast, Okta, or CrowdStrike?"

Answer:

"Those categories are important, but they protect different layers. Mimecast protects email and collaboration risk. Okta manages identity and access. CrowdStrike protects endpoints and workloads. Cyber Sentinels focuses on operational trust inside sensitive workflows after access or participation begins: hiring interviews, verification sessions, agent activity, governance review, replay, and receipts."

If the buyer already uses those tools, position Cyber Sentinels as complementary:

"We are not asking you to replace those systems. We are creating the trust chronology around the workflow so talent, security, compliance, and leadership can work from the same evidence."

## Demo discipline

- Keep the story linear.
- Avoid abstract category language unless the buyer asks.
- Use "Flags" instead of "Signals".
- Use "Verification Evidence" instead of "Provenance".
- Use "Verification Chronology" instead of "Trust Fabric".
- Use "Governance Review" instead of abstract orchestration.
- Avoid perfect detection claims.
- Avoid surveillance framing.
